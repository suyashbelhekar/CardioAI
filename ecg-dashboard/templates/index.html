Understanding Cardiac Arrhythmia
Key facts every cardiologist should know
What is Arrhythmia?
An arrhythmia is an irregular heartbeat — the heart beats too fast, too slow, or with an irregular pattern due to faulty electrical signals.
Global Prevalence
Over 1.5 billion people worldwide are affected. Atrial fibrillation alone affects 37 million and is the most common sustained arrhythmia.
Risk Factors
Coronary artery disease, hypertension, diabetes, obesity, sleep apnea, excessive alcohol, and advancing age are the primary risk factors.
Early Detection
Early ECG screening can prevent stroke, heart failure, and sudden cardiac death. AI tools enable faster, consistent screening at scale.
Arrhythmia Types Detected
CardioAI classifies 11 distinct cardiac rhythm patterns
NSR
Normal Sinus Rhythm
Regular rhythm from the sinus node. Heart beats normally between 60–100 bpm.
Normal Risk
SBR
Sinus Bradycardia
Heart rate below 60 bpm. Common in athletes; may indicate conduction issues.
Mild Risk
STach
Sinus Tachycardia
Heart rate above 100 bpm. Often caused by stress, fever, or dehydration.
Mild Risk
PAC
Premature Atrial Contractions
Extra beats from the atria. Usually benign but may indicate AFib risk.
Mild Risk
PVC
Premature Ventricular Contractions
Extra beats from the ventricles. Frequent PVCs require cardiology evaluation.
Moderate Risk
SVT
Supraventricular Tachycardia
Rapid rhythm above the ventricles. May require medication or catheter ablation.
Moderate Risk
AFib
Atrial Fibrillation
Chaotic atrial activity with irregular RR intervals. Increases stroke risk 5×.
High Risk
VT
Ventricular Tachycardia
Rapid ventricular rhythm — potentially life-threatening. Requires urgent care.
High Risk
Block
Heart Block
Delayed or blocked AV conduction. High-degree block may require a pacemaker.
Moderate Risk
Paced
Paced Rhythm
Heartbeats generated by an implanted pacemaker device. Regular follow-up needed.
Normal Risk
Bigeminy
Ventricular Bigeminy
Every other beat is a PVC (N-V-N-V pattern). Requires Holter monitoring.
Moderate Risk
